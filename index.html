01Why these exist

The tools the phone deserved

I

The pocket supercomputer.

You carry the most powerful device ever made, and the creative tools it got are mostly gimmicks — apps that demo well, teach nothing, and keep nothing you'd want back. That gap is the reason Ferric exists.

II

Designed by artists.

Each instrument is built by working musicians as the exact tool we needed to create, learn, and explore — not a feature roadmap. If a control doesn't help you make something, it isn't on the panel.

III

You, in the driver's seat.

Nothing here makes the music for you. They are instruments — you play them, and playing is how they teach. Never touched one and know no theory? The labelled panel shows you what a filter or a bias control actually does, the way hardware always has. Twenty years in? The same controls go deep enough to find sounds nobody has made. One tool, both players — no toy mode.

IV

Constraints, on purpose.

Built for the phone deliberately: a small screen forces decisions, and decisions finish songs. It's always in your pocket, so the idea at the bus stop can be a recorded track by the time you're home.

02Material study

Every machine owes an elder

These four are why the family looks the way it does — a blackface combo, a cassette deck that made bedroom records possible, a sampler built for chopping, a slider synth that never needed a manual. We're not re-creating them. We keep what earned its keep — the panel order, the labels, the feel under a hand — and add what only the phone could ever do.

And they love the hardware back.

These aren't a replacement for the gear on your desk — they're its missing half. Wire your synth in over MIDI and load patches and patterns designed on the phone. Connect your drum machine and trade loops and packs with it. A symbiotic rig: the phone makes the hardware do more, and the hardware makes the phone sound real. A natural evolution in sound, not a museum of it.

04The kit

They play together

Every instrument runs in the background, so two or more can sound at once — a synth over a sampler over a tape machine, all on one phone. They keep one clock: MIDI clock inside the phone, Ableton Link across the room. Start a pattern on the P–16 and the J–17 falls in step; a friend's laptop joins the jam from the couch.

And they share one shelf. L–1 is the kit's library — every sound, pack, and patch any instrument makes, named by you, on every device you own. The tuner checks it, the sampler chops it, the synth voices it, the amp colors it, the tape commits it. One kit. Everything you need to record a track, in your pocket.

06The whole shelf

A full studio in your pocket, under fifty dollars

This is what the family is for. Not six apps that happen to share a look — every tool you need to make a finished record, designed to work as one instrument. Sample and chop it, play it, amp it, tune it, track it, keep it. Nothing else to buy, nothing else to learn.

For an aspiring producer, that's the whole education in one place: a real signal chain you can see, panels labelled like the hardware they came from, and the freedom to try anything without breaking something expensive. For a working artist, it's the dream rig reduced to what fits in a pocket.

Four machines at $9.99, two that cost nothing — the whole shelf for less than the price of one plugin, and less than a single month of most studio subscriptions.

Anything you can imagine, built and recorded on the phone already in your hand. That was the promise of this whole technology age. We're just building the part that got skipped.

07The deal

Priced like a tool, not a trap

An instrument should be worth more than it costs, and it should be obvious who it's for. These are priced so a kid with a used phone can own the whole shelf — and built so that trying something wild never costs you anything but time.

  • BUY ONCE A machine is a purchase, never a subscription. Nothing expires, nothing needs renewing, and revisions are free.
  • NO DATA, EVER We don't collect your data. No analytics, no telemetry, no account. Your sounds and recordings stay on your device — we never see them, and never want to.
  • YOUR WORK IS YOURS Plain files in the open — WAV, MIDI, folders you can read in Finder. Nothing locked in a format only we can open.
  • WORKS OFFLINE No server check, no phone-home. If the shop goes quiet tomorrow, every instrument you own keeps working.
  • WHAT YOU PAY BUILDS Every purchase funds the next machine — more instruments, deeper integrations, and the family growing into what it should be.

No dark patterns, no upsell screens, no manufactured scarcity. We're not trying to scheme money out of anybody — we're trying to build the instruments we wished existed, and sell them at a price that keeps the lights on.
