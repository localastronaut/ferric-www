01Why it exists

The kit needs one shelf.

Five machines each printing sounds into their own corner is five corners. Everything a Ferric app prints lands in one iCloud folder — takes, kits, patches, rigs, song shapes — and L–1 is the room that walks it. The crate is the fast lane; the library is the deep shelf.

It's a shelf, not a leash. No database, no index, no proprietary container: rename in Finder, refile in Finder, back it up in Finder — L–1 reads the folder as it stands and never argues with it. If L–1 vanished tomorrow, every file would still be exactly where you left it, in a format something else can open.

And it's furniture, not an instrument. It makes no sound of its own — it hands sounds to the things that do, and it can never change or remove one you have.

“Loading is not keeping: the sound arrives, the faders are yours, and SAVE is still how it becomes one of your own.”

— the L–1 field manual

Digging

Tap a sound to hear it; tap again to stop. DEAL pulls a random handful when you don't know what you want. The RING is the crate you fill while digging, and BUILD KIT lays it down as sixteen numbered pads — for the P–16, or the real 404.

A take is a folder

When an instrument prints a take it writes the sound, the line you played, and the recipe that made the sound — one folder, three ordinary files. The MIDI still drags into your DAW; the patch still opens in the synth. The folder groups them, it does not seal them.

Keeping

A star is a real Finder tag, coloured yellow, visible in Finder and Spotlight. OPEN IN J–17 hands a patch back to the synth that made it; OPEN IN D–65 hands a rig back to the amp — the verb only appears when the machine is on this device.

04How it's used

The desk organises; the phone digs

  1. BROWSEThree sections — LIBRARY is the shelf you dig, INSTRUMENTS is what the Ferric apps printed, HARDWARE is what your machines made. Search only ever searches where you stand.
  2. AUDITIONTap to hear, tap to stop. Cloud sounds pull down and play the moment they land.
  3. ORGANIZEStar it, deal a handful, fill the ring, build a kit. Any folder you add becomes an aisle.
  4. HAND IT OVERSend to the crate for right now, or open a patch in the machine that made it. Start a song from a form — a name, an artist, a shape, and nothing else.
05In the kit

The kit's shared memory

WHO PRINTS

Every machine gets its own drawer — J–17 patches and takes, D–65 rigs, P–16 kits, T–6 forms. The shelf can hand you a sound, and can never change or remove one you have.

EVERY DEVICE

One shelf on Mac, iPhone and iPad. The desk is a big resizable librarian; the phone is a quick portrait digger. Apple carries the folder; L–1 never syncs anything itself.

THE HARDWARE

A machine is a folder — drop a JU-06A pack into HARDWARE and its patches, chords and patterns appear. Build a kit and carry it to the 404 on its own SD card.

THE SONGS

Forms are song shapes with their guides beside them. START strikes the folder, copies the template, writes the tempo — and the pocket demo tape carries your newest bounces to the phone.

No Audio Unit, on purpose — L–1 is furniture, not an instrument. It makes no sound of its own.
