01Why it exists

One amp, modeled deep.

Not a menu of amps — a '65 blackface Deluxe with the real AB763 tone network, component values and interactions and all. Past 5 the power section starts to give: supply sag, then push-pull grit. That push is the amp, not a pedal. There's no mid knob because the Deluxe never had one, and the loaded mid scoop is the sound.

The board teaches what pedals actually do. Fuzz into compressor is a different animal than compressor into fuzz — the order is part of the sound, and you learn it by dragging tiles. If a pedal here makes you go read about spring reverbs and buy a real tank, the pedal worked.

And it grows: a new pedal lands every month at $4.99 — a place or an object rendered as DSP and a faceplate, bought once, on the shelf forever. The baseline board is complete on its own; nobody buys D–65 and finds a demo.

03The field manual

Plug in. Stomp. Strum. Print.

The head

Eight knobs and the jewel on the blackface strip, every one sweeping -135° to +135° like a skirted Fender knob. Set VOLUME for the tone, MASTER for the neighbors. REVERB is how much spring tank you hear; DWELL is how hard the springs are driven — crank it and they bark. The tremolo is bias tremolo, ahead of the clip, breathing with the breakup.
DOUBLE-TAP SENDS ANY KNOB HOME

The board

Eight velcro slots; signal snakes in reading order, top row first. Fuzz into compressor is a different animal than compressor into fuzz — the order is part of the sound. Everything stays warm when bypassed: no clicks, and the delay's repeats ring out. A bare board is just you and the amp.
+ HOLD A TILE AND SLIDE IT — ORDER SAVES WITH THE RIG

The bench

Tap the input jack and the bench Telecaster plugs in — the hidden way to hear the whole rig with no cable, no interface, no guitar. Tap picks; drag along a string and it slides; a sweep is a strum that rakes every string it crosses, clean, in order.

The tape

A four-minute ring, post-cab, catching everything the amp puts out whether or not you meant to keep it. Set IN and OUT, tap the tray — a stereo WAV plus its rig sidecar, and one tap on any take's rig button puts you back in the sound that made it.
IN → OUT · THE FULL TAKE STAYS ON THE REEL

The whole manual ships inside the app — the folded sheet in the amp's tolex pocket. Tap the wordmark.

04How it's used

Guitar to take, one cable

  1. PLUG INGuitar into the interface, interface into the phone. The green bar means signal at the jack. No interface? The bench Telecaster is one tap away.
  2. SET THE AMPDial the strip. Volume past 5 is where the amp gives; the bright cap fades as you climb, exactly like the hardware.
  3. BUILD THE BOARDBring pedals down off the shelf, drag them into order, stomp the strip below the hairline.
  4. PLAY~12 ms jack to ear. The jewel catches your signal and breathes with the playing.
  5. PRINTThe good part is already on the tape. Trim it, print it — the WAV lands in Takes with the rig that made it.
05The pedal drops

New gear every month. Take only what moves you.

The board you buy is finished: amp, compressor, overdrive, fuzz, chorus, delay and the looper. That's the $9.99, and it never changes. Nobody buys D–65 and finds a demo.

Then, once a month, something new lands on the shelf — $4.99, à la carte, yours the moment you take it. Skip three and take the fourth. It isn't a subscription and there's nothing to miss; a pedal you didn't buy in March is still there in December. The pedals are where the cadence starts — as the shop can keep up, the other instruments get drops of their own: kits for the sampler, patch banks for the synth, reels for the deck.

These are the strange ones — the boxes you'd never drop three hundred dollars on just to find out. A quartz gate built from a 1969 Tokyo chronograph. A mineral spring that holds you under and vents a geyser when you let go. Forty kilometers of camel-stride tremolo across a stereo desert. Five dollars to find out whether a strange idea is your new sound.

And none of it is only for guitar. Run a vocal through the fuzz, a synth pad through the springs, a drum loop through the chronograph — D–65 is an effects rack that happens to have a great amp in it. Every pedal is drawn to the same standard as the faceplate: real knobs, real lettering, a lamp that means something. A pedal should be worth looking at while you use it.

QZ–69 CHRONOGRAPH

A quartz gate from the Tokyo chronograph, 1969. The week is the pattern — lit days pass signal, dark days close the gate, quartz-steady.

HS–95 HOT SPRINGS

The Jacumba soak — mineral water at 95°F. Press the water and it holds you under; let go and the spring vents a geyser. It trails through bypass.

CV–40 SAHARA

Forty kilometers a day. Pace is the camel's stride, sway leans the stereo field, mirage is the oud's twin string rising to full heat-haze.
