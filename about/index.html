The bench

For years
I thought I wasn't
a musician.

I was wrong, but it took me most of my life to find out. Ferric is the set of instruments that would have got me there sooner — built in one room in San Diego, by one person, for anyone still standing where I was.

01The long way in

How I got here

I have loved music for as long as I can remember. Shows when I was a kid, emo and punk records, and later the festivals and albums that rearranged what I thought was possible.

I just could not make any of it. Every time I picked up a guitar it sounded terrible. Every time I sat down at a piano it was painful to listen to. Singing — worse. So I decided the matter was settled: some people make music, and I was someone who loves it. That was going to be my part.

I still went to every band practice a friend would let me sit in on. Watching people build something out of nothing never stopped being the coolest thing I had ever seen.

Some people make music, and I was someone who loves it. That was going to be my part.

A screen full of engineering

In college I got into electronic music and had friends who were DJs. That was the first time I saw Ableton, and it was mesmerizing — and completely opaque. It looked like a piece of engineering I was never going to understand. I downloaded it anyway, got a MIDI keyboard and a launcher, and tried, on and off, for years. It never stuck.

Somewhere in there I got an acoustic guitar and learned a handful of chords. I could never hold attention long enough to learn other people's songs, so I learned shapes and made things up instead. None of it was good. It was still the best part of my week.

I made strange little things in GarageBand, tried to learn Logic, pieced samples together into stuff I never played for anyone. Nothing stuck there either.

A hotel room, and one last try

During COVID I got stuck in a hotel room for a week while travelling, and decided to give Ableton one final honest attempt. A week of nothing else — tutorials, samples, apps, every hour I had. And at the end of it, for the first time, something I actually thought was good.

What had been stopping me

It was not talent. It was that my brain only works when it can see the whole system. I need to know how everything connects before any single piece means anything — and nobody had ever shown me the whole picture. Just isolated instruments, one at a time, each one a separate wall to fail at.

I also did not have a band. There was never anybody consistently around to record with. So I had to learn all of it myself: drums, bass, guitar, keys, vocals. I never got great at any single one of them. But learning all of them is what finally taught me how music actually fits together.

It started with a tuner

I have been writing software since I was a kid. So the first thing I built was the simplest thing I actually needed: a tuner. No ads, no subscription, no upsell screen. Turn it on, tune the guitar, done.

Then I thought it should show me the scale. Then it should detect the key. Then the chords that belong to it. And so on.

The sampler started the same way. I wanted to learn the SP-404, so I built a replica to understand it — and once I understood it, I started adding the things I wished it did.

A few weeks later I looked at what was on my phone and realized I had built every instrument I had ever wanted. Not in a studio. In my pocket — on planes, in hotel rooms, wherever an idea turned up.

02What it's for

Lower the door, not the ceiling

My goal with Ferric is to introduce people to music in a way that does not require you to already be a musician. I am not going to pretend the work disappears — getting deep at any of this still takes time and real effort, and it should. But the door should not be the hard part, and for me it was the hard part for twenty years.

SHOW THE WHOLE SYSTEM

The instruments are a connected kit on purpose. Seeing how tuning, playing, amping and recording fit together is the thing that finally made music make sense to me.

NO WALL AT THE DOOR

Labelled panels, no menus, no manuals you have to survive first. Pick it up, make a sound, and learn what the control does by turning it.

THEN GO DEEPER

If one of these sends you off to buy a real amp, a real synth, a real four-track — or to finally finish something that matters to you — it worked.

03The proof

These are practice tools for a real project

I release music as SALTON — desert psych, atmospheric, made on exactly this gear. The records are the demo. If the tools were not good enough to finish real songs on, I would find out long before you did.
