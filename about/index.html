Ferric is one person and a laptop, across a lot of years of chasing sounds, picking up instruments, and making things in rooms that weren't studios. It's the kit I kept wishing existed — so I built it, one machine at a time.

01Why it exists

Built out of what was missing

I've loved music my whole life, and for a long stretch of it I couldn't make any. Every guitar I picked up sounded wrong, every piano hurt to listen to, and I quietly assumed that settled the question.

I got in through the side door instead. My first real venture into music was at Red Bull Records, and after that I started a music app to help musicians. Then I burned out and left all of it — the label world, the app, the whole thing.

What brought me back was making music with nothing attached to it. No launch, no users, no plan. Just for me.

And that's when it finally clicked — not because of talent, but because I could see the whole picture at last. I never had a band, so I had to learn all of it myself: drums, bass, guitar, keys, vocals. I never got great at any one of them, but learning all of them is what taught me how music actually fits together.

I've also been writing software since I was a kid. So when something was missing, I built it instead of waiting.

It started with a tuner. I just wanted one that turned on and tuned my guitar — no ads, no subscription, no upsell.

Then it should show me the scale. Then detect the key. Then the chords that belong to it. The sampler began the same way: I wanted to learn the SP-404, so I built a replica to understand it, and once I understood it I added the parts I wished it had.

A few weeks later I looked at my phone and realised I'd built every instrument I had been wishing for. Not in a studio — in my pocket, on planes, in hotel rooms, wherever the idea turned up. That's the whole thing. Ferric is that kit, made properly.

It's also why it's built the way it is. No subscriptions, no accounts, nothing that needs you to come back daily — I have already done the version of this that burns a person out, and I am not doing it again, to you or to me.

02What it's for

Lower the door, not the ceiling

The goal is to lower the barrier to getting in. Not to pretend the work disappears — getting deep at any of this takes real time and real effort, and it should. But the way in shouldn't be the hard part, and for a long time that's exactly what it was.

SHOW THE WHOLE SYSTEM

The instruments are a connected kit on purpose. Seeing how tuning, playing, amping and recording fit together is the thing that finally made music make sense to me.

NO WALL AT THE DOOR

Labelled panels, no menus, no manuals you have to survive first. Pick it up, make a sound, and learn what the control does by turning it.

THEN GO DEEPER

If one of these sends you off to buy a real amp, a real synth, a real four-track — or to finally finish something that matters to you — it worked.

03The proof

I make my records on these

These are not sketchpads. I use them on my own releases — writing, jamming, tracking live. Simple enough to knock out a beat in five minutes, serious enough to run the amp into a real PA and play a room through it, and good enough to finish a studio-quality track recorded entirely on a phone, an iPad or a Mac.

I release music as SALTON — desert psych, atmospheric, made on exactly this gear. The records are the demo. If the tools weren't good enough to finish real songs on, I'd find out long before you did.
